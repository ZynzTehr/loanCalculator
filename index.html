Financial Literacy Guide

Understand key borrowing concepts to make informed, confident financial decisions.

Amortization is the process of spreading out a loan into equal payments over time. In early payments, a higher percentage goes toward interest because the remaining balance is large. As the balance decreases, a larger portion of each payment goes directly toward paying down the principal.

The interest rate is the base percentage charged by the lender for borrowing money. APR (Annual Percentage Rate) includes the interest rate plus mandatory lender fees (such as origination fees), giving you a more complete picture of the total annual cost of borrowing.

A down payment reduces the total principal you borrow upfront. Because interest is calculated on remaining principal, a larger down payment immediately lowers both your monthly payment and the total cumulative interest paid over the life of the loan.

  • Mortgages: Property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA dues, closing costs (2%–5%), and PMI (if down payment is under 20%).
  • Auto Loans: State sales tax, vehicle registration/title fees, dealer documentation fees, and mandatory full-coverage insurance.
  • Personal Loans: Origination fees (1%–8%) deducted upfront from payout, and late payment penalty fees.
  • Student Loans: Interest capitalization during in-school or deferment periods and loan origination fees.
